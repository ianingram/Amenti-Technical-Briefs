Handoff · the reciter, the certificate, and what the emerald is for

TheLiving Voice

Every spell before this one is about the dead

One hundred and ninety spells, and every one of them is spoken for the dead — recited over the body, written on the coffin, addressed to gatekeepers on behalf of somebody who can no longer speak. The reading room holds the same arrangement: a thousand figures, every one of them silent, their words preserved and unable to be uttered by the mouths that wrote them.

Read-aloud inverts it. A living person puts breath through a dead person's sentence. That is not a bonus mechanic bolted to a quiz. It is the only place in this entire system where the direction of the transaction reverses — where the seeker gives something to the record rather than taking something from it.

This brief is about building it to be worth that.

I · Where it stands

The reciter works and is honest about what it is. Its own header states the privacy position plainly: audio is NEVER captured or stored — recognition processes it live and we send only a coverage RATIO. That was the right call and it stays.

The half it did not flag is worse. startHonor() exists because Safari and Firefox have no SpeechRecognition, and it pays the full reward on a timer paced at roughly 360ms a word. No microphone is required. On those browsers, reading aloud is waiting aloud — and that is not an exploit anybody has to discover, it is the default behaviour on most of the phones in the world.

Three readings follow from that table and each one is a decision, not a tuning knob.

The quiz faucet is already correct — and correct by accident
The unique index emerald_ledger_once means a question pays once per seeker, ever. Lifetime quiz minting is therefore bounded at roughly 16,520 ◈ and cannot be farmed. That index was written for correctness rather than for economics, and it happens to encode the Book's own logic: you cannot pass the same gate twice. Leave it exactly as it is.
Read-aloud is the only unbounded faucet, and it out-pays thinking

The curve resets daily, per topic. Fifty-nine topics is 2,832 ◈ a day for one determined person — on the order of a million a year. Meanwhile a complete quiz, six questions answered and two arguments filed for the docket, pays 280.

Reading aloud currently pays roughly ten times better than thinking, every single day. That does not promote reading aloud. It makes everything else pointless, and it is the one number in the economy that cannot survive contact with a thousand users.

The pool does not scale, and it scales backwards

POOL_SIZE is 500 ◈ a week regardless of how many people exist. One seeker takes 500. A thousand seekers split 500. The reward for arguing therefore collapses precisely as the hall fills — and the 500 is minted from nothing, so it is 26,000 ◈ of pure annual issuance whether or not anyone argued.

III · Four decisions

1 · The pool is the stake, not a subsidy

A vote burns 5 ◈. Settlement mints 500 ◈. Those are two unrelated flows, and joining them closes the loop: the pool is what the hall actually staked, redistributed.

Keep POOL_SIZE as a floor for thin weeks if a floor is wanted. But a pool conjured from nothing is not a weighing. On 14 August the court sealed its first case on zero votes and distributed nothing — and that was the system behaving correctly. The parimutuel makes that the rule rather than the accident.

2 · Honour mode acknowledges. It does not mint.

Two options and no third. Either those browsers get real capture via MediaRecorder — which works everywhere SpeechRecognition does not — or honour mode records the read and pays nothing. A timer must never be a faucet.

3 · Cap the day, and invert the curve

Keep the daily reset. Repetition is the point — it is practice, and practice is what a reading site is for. But two changes:

NowProposedWhy
Daily ceilingnone~60 ◈ across all topics three careful passages earn what fifty-nine ground ones do
Curve20 · 12 · 8 · 5 · 3rising, not falling the fifth read of one passage is the one that is improving. A falling curve pays for showing up; a rising one pays for fluency.

4 · Verify without storing

Keeping every reading is expensive and most readings will not be good. So: cache only, and the seeker keeps their own. The audio need not be stored to be checked — it needs to reach the Worker once. Score it there, discard the audio, keep the number. That closes the soft spot the fleet already named, and it costs one request rather than one object per read, forever.

IV · The certificate

This is the piece that changes what the emerald is for, and it is the reason the rest of this brief is worth doing.

It is the Negative Confession, in reverse
Spell 125 is forty-two declarations of what was not done. The certificate is the affirmative form, and it is declared by the record rather than by the seeker: I read this passage aloud. I cleared eighty-four per cent. I answered six of six on the Rubicon. I staked twelve emeralds on arguments that were not my own. Same structure, opposite sign — and both are statements of the conditions for legitimate passage.

The audio is the proof. The metadata is the claim. A file that says some audio is a voice memo. A file that says this reading, of this passage, by this seeker, on this date, at this coverage is checkable, and it is the thing a person actually keeps.

Two things to get right before the shape is fixed

Sign it. The mint already holds the primitive — makeSession and readSession HMAC with the service key. The same primitive signs a certificate, and a seeker's claim then verifies without the Worker surrendering anything private. An unsigned certificate is a text file anybody can write.

Do not let it de-anonymise the hall. /hall publishes arguments and vote counts with no author attached, deliberately. A seeker publishing their own participation is theirs to publish — but the format must not make it easy to correlate a certificate back to the anonymous rows. Decide the field list with that in mind, not afterwards.

And the format itself: audio plus a JSON sidecar is honest and ugly. Audio plus a rendered card — terminal standard, the figure's plate, the numbers set in Share Tech Mono — is the thing somebody shares. The plates, the fonts and the whole visual language already exist.

V · What this does to the economics

It makes them almost incidental, which is the point.

Three rewards, three instruments — instead of one doing all three badly
The record acknowledges. Every read that clears coverage writes a row — passages read, days running, texts finished aloud. Uncapped, unfarmable, and worth more to a serious reader than the currency.

The emerald pays for what is scarce. Not the read — the record already acknowledged that. The first complete read of a text, a return after a gap, coverage well above the floor. Small numbers that stay meaningful at ten thousand users.

The certificate is the reason. Your own voice, on a dead person's sentence, with the record attached and signed. It costs nothing to issue and it is worth more than any payout.

VII · Open, and not answered here

  • Is 512 a cap or a reading? It is 2⁹, which looks deliberate — but nothing in the code enforces it, and one seeker sweeping the library mints thirty-two times that. If it is a cap, the whole issuance schedule is two orders of magnitude out. If it is a measurement, then what needs designing is the rate. Nothing else in this brief can be finally costed until that is settled.
  • Does a great reading get offered a home? Cache-only by default — but a read that clears a high bar could be invited into the reading room. One voice per passage, chosen. Rare enough to cost nothing, and it turns reading aloud into something that can be won rather than ground.
  • Coverage is a weak instrument. 0.80 of the words, in order, with a three-word look-ahead. It measures neither pace, nor pauses, nor whether the reading was any good. It is the right floor for a read happened. It is not a measure of a reading worth keeping, and step 5 will want one.
