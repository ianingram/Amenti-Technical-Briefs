Brief No. 2

One Job at a Time

20 November 2026Amenti Studios · Storyboard workflowStatus: in force; deployed and committed

Google's refusals were caused mostly by us: up to five storyboard runs drawing at once against one shared picture quota. The cure is discipline first and machinery second. No more than three runs draw at once, each picture waits longer before it gives up, and when Vertex is full the Worker now tries three more regions and then our own Tier 2 AI Studio key.

The problem in one chart

On the night of 19–20 November the Dracula batches for XX to XXIV went out in quick succession. The early ones ran one or two at a time and almost every beat came back. Later, at 06:18–06:36 UTC, four batches overlapped; at 07:00–07:17 UTC, five did, all of them ours. Refusals climbed with the number of runs at once.

Why more runs meant more refusals

  • Pictures share one quota. Every storyboard picture is drawn by Vertex AI on Google's shared capacity for the image model. Five runs each sending pictures back to back looks to Google like one very busy customer, and it answers with 429 (too many requests) or 503 (busy).
  • The old retries gave up too soon. A refused picture had fewer tries with shorter waits, mostly inside the same busy minutes, and then the beat was marked empty.
  • There was nowhere else to go. The Worker sent every picture to one place, Vertex's global endpoint. When that was full, the picture failed.
  • It was not the new tier. The Tier 2 increase applies to the AI Studio key, which until now only the voice used. The pictures never touched it, so the higher limit could not help them, until this change.

1 · The throttle: three at most

A new step at the start of every storyboard run asks GitHub how many earlier storyboard runs are drawing. If three are, it waits 30 seconds and asks again, for up to two hours. Runs keep their place in line: the oldest waiting run goes next.

Three is a ceiling, not a target. Ian's rule for the working day is one job at a time: one chapter's batch, checked, then the next. The throttle only guarantees that a busy moment, or another chat's runs, can never stack up to five again.

GitHub's own "concurrency group" was tried first and removed the same hour: it keeps only one run waiting and silently cancels the rest, which would have lost batches.

2 · Patience: retries that outlast a busy minute

A refused picture is now tried up to ten times. The wait before each new try grows by 30 seconds, to a ceiling of three minutes, so the tries spread across about twenty minutes instead of one busy minute. A dropped connection while reading Google's answer no longer stops the run.

3 · The new roads, and where Tier 2 comes in

The Worker's picture route (/storyboard/image) no longer has a single destination. When a road answers 429, 503 or 404 it takes the next one; any other answer, good or bad, is final. The last road is our own AI Studio key, now on Tier 2, the same key the voice has used as its fallback since 19 November.

What it costs

  • Time. A batch may wait for a slot, and a refused beat may take up to twenty minutes to come back instead of failing at once. Batches finish later, but a redraw round is not needed.
  • Money. Nothing new on the Vertex roads. Pictures that reach AI Studio are billed there at its own rate; the roads report shows how many.
  • Risk. The voice and the pictures now share the AI Studio key in a pinch. Pictures reach it only after four Vertex roads are busy, so the voice keeps nearly all of it.

Recommendation

Keep the rule and the machinery as they are. Redraw XXIV's refused beats one batch at a time and read the roads in the log: if most still come back from vertex:global, the throttle alone was the cure.

Open questions

  • After a week of road reports, should the ceiling stay at three or drop to two, the level that never failed?
  • Should the agents' books (Musketeers, Gallic War) be bound by the same slot count, so that all chats share the three slots?
  • Should a monthly cap be set on AI Studio picture spending, so the voice's fallback is always left room?

Brief No. 1

Storyboarding

18 November 2026Amenti StudiosStatus: in force; the quality goal is open

For now Google's image model is the storyboard and the establishing camera, and Midjourney paints the people. The goal is one painter we can automate end to end: Google at Midjourney's quality. Until a blind test says it is there, the split stands.

What changed in the last sessions

  • The split. Google (Nano Banana 2.1, through the storyboard workflow) draws establishing shots, exteriors, grounds, rooftops, far views and the scene seen through a lit window from outside, and makes small repairs: a beard removed, tears softened. Midjourney, run by Ian, paints close and medium shots of people.
  • Camera rhythm. Each chapter is planned in shot sizes before drawing. A new place opens on an establishing shot; long narration sits on far shots ("the eye is less critical"); close-ups are kept for a few moments; a chapter stuck in one room steps outside every scene or two.
  • Light. Day scenes are painted brilliant, as the counterweight to the dark chapters; night keeps Book I's tone.
  • The face check comes first. Every picture is checked against the cast portraits before it is placed, not after Ian finds the fault.
  • Recording by machine. The offline mixer records whole books on GitHub with the render key. This was built on 14 November; the rulebook still said otherwise until 18 November.
  • Running a tight ship. Skills are the rulebook and are updated in the same sitting as any change of method; a logbook entry closes every session; a weekly audit (Sundays, 6:40 p.m.) compares the skills with the docs and the workflows and flags drift.

Agents: what they add

On 18 November two agents took a whole book each, unattended: The Three Musketeers and Caesar's Gallic War, from library text to cast, cue sheets, sound and score, style plate, chapter 1 pictures, intro and voicing.

The lesson of the night: an agent copies whatever the rulebook says. A stale rule ("only Ian's Safari can record") would have been copied into both books. Hence the same-sitting skill updates and the weekly audit.

Why we went back to Midjourney

  • The evidence. In chapter XVII Google drew 23 close and medium shots that followed the text well and kept every face on model. Ian rejected them as "too commercial": smooth surfaces, finished faces, the look of a book cover. A second pass styled on the Book I paintings moved the light and colour a long way, but the faces stayed illustrated.
  • What Midjourney has. Loose brushwork, real shadow, faces painted rather than rendered. Side by side with the Midjourney picture of Mina and Jonathan sorting the papers, the gap was plain.
  • What it costs. Every prompt is pasted by hand. Relax mode refuses permutations and ran about one prompt an hour on 17 November. It gives Seward a beard even with his portrait as reference. Session zips with the same name overwrote each other.
  • What Google does better. Following the text, keeping faces consistent across a chapter, repairs by instruction, and landscapes and architecture: Ian, of the exteriors, "holding up well".

The goal: one painter we can automate

Google's model has an official API and runs on the account that already voices the books, so it can run all night inside our workflows. Midjourney has no API and must not be automated. The aim is Google at Midjourney's level for close and medium shots, so a whole chapter can be painted without hand work. The experiments, cheapest first:

  1. Midjourney paintings as the style reference. Feed Google our best Midjourney takes, not Google-made pictures, as the style image, so it copies painted faces and brushwork. Cost: nothing new.
  2. A repaint pass. Draw the scene for composition and faces, then edit it with an instruction to repaint in loose oil with visible strokes and broken edges. Two calls per picture, about 7¢.
  3. Larger output. 2K instead of 1K (about 5¢ instead of 3.4¢) gives more surface for brushwork. Needs a small Worker change, which waits for Ian's OK.
  4. Newer models as they come. Test each new Google image model on the same scenes the day it is available.
  5. A blind test, as we did for the voices. Twenty close shots, Google and Midjourney mixed and unlabelled; Ian picks the better of each pair. When Google wins about half, it takes over people scenes for that book.

Recommendation

Keep the split for Dracula Book III and the new books' people scenes. Run experiments 1 and 2 on one chapter of The Three Musketeers this week, and the blind test when they look close. Approve the 2K change if the repaint pass is promising.

Open questions

  • Approve the 2K Worker change now, or after experiments 1 and 2?
  • Which twenty scenes for the blind test: Dracula, so Midjourney's best is the benchmark, or a new book?
  • Should the agents paint people scenes in Google as placeholders until Midjourney takes arrive, or leave those beats to far shots?
