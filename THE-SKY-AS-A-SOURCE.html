The Sky as a Source

A night's work on the timeline, and the claim underneath it: the planets are a primary source in the fullest sense — the only witness that is still testifying.

Livy reaches you through a translator. Saturn reaches you through arithmetic anyone can redo and no one can slant.
— the argument this brief is built on

The claim

The planets are the record; the ephemeris is only its edition.

A figure's room holds what they wrote. A planet's room holds what it did — its own path, still legible. That makes the sky the most primary source on the ship. Every human account — Livy, Josephus, Newton — is filtered through a person, a language, an era. The planets are unmediated: Caesar's own words reach you through Whiston or Motte; Saturn's rising reaches you through a calculation that anyone can repeat and no one can slant.

It answers the citation policy's own test — can a reader reach the identical thing again? For Livy, they reach the edition. For the planets, they reach the sky itself: recompute it, or wait and watch it happen. It is the one source that can never go out of print.

The organizing principle

Not “how rare” — “who could see it.”

The sky sorts by a single honest axis: how much of the Earth witnessed the event. Each mark then claims exactly what it can defend, no more.

worldwide
Conjunctions, gatherings, Halley. Seen across the whole night hemisphere at once — China, Cairo and Cahokia the same night. No location claim needed: it happened is it was seen. These are the events that genuinely connected all of humanity at a moment.
latitude-local
Due-east risings over Giza. “Rises due east” is a statement about one horizon; from another latitude the same planet rises at another bearing. Honestly labelled over Giza, because the Great Pyramid is aligned to true north within arcminutes — the monument itself defines the line.
track-local
Eclipses — deliberately NOT on the ship. Visible only along a narrow shadow-path, and the path is the meaning. ΔT — the drift between clock time and the Earth's rotation — is hours before 1500, so the date is computable but the place is not. An eclipse with an unreliable location is worse than useless, because location is its entire content. They belong in the events register as recorded sightings, where the chronicler who watched the sky go dark is the source.

The rhythm

The conjunction is the ship's clock — not its meaning.

The great conjunction returns every ~20 years like a metronome, but unlike a drawn grid line it is a real event people watched and marked. Five rings to a century; count 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and you have walked 200 years. The century is the downbeat — a brighter full-height rule the eye lands on, and the conjunction nearest it drawn filled, so the count arrives visibly.

But the rhythm is a clock, not an oracle. Tested against the roster, the great conjunctions land on exactly as many souls as randomly chosen years — 33 of 143 against 32 expected. Show a cycle as an anchor; never let a surface imply it means anything. The rings time the journey; they do not explain the passengers.

The marks

Each body drawn as itself.

  • Planet risings — the astronomical glyph in the planet's own colour: ♆ Neptune, ♅ Uranus, ♄ Saturn, ♃ Jupiter. The colours anyone knows from a photograph, so the row reads before it is explained.
  • Conjunctions — a gold ring where the two planets touch. Not a glyph: the event is a meeting, and the ring says so.
  • Gatherings — the rarest mark on the ship, four coloured orbs stacked, on a full-height beam with the year beside it. You cannot pass one without seeing it, which is right for something that happens 14 times in five thousand years.
  • Halley's Comet — an ice-blue comet with a short tail on the sky row, drawn from Page2's own curated perihelion list. It sparkles only at close zoom — a reward for arriving on a return, not a permanent twinkle. The mark says returns, not visible from Giza: astronomically true, modest claim.

The instrument

From a chart you read to an instrument you interrogate.

The timeline places a figure in a fixed 200-year window centred on their midpoint, ordered by death year to agree with Page2's helix. Both axes scroll — time across, population down — and the vertical now follows the window, gliding to the band of souls alive in view so the names populate as you travel through the centuries.

A side panel gives the figure's world: the events of their life, the sky over Giza in their years, and who was alive beside them — ordered rooms first, then by overlap, so the most relevant contemporaries rise. Proper names in terminal blue, so a reader scans for who and where without reading every line.

Anything clicked broadcasts its span, and everything sharing it lights up. — the connection gesture

Click a life and everyone who overlapped it glows; click an event and the people alive when it happened; click a conjunction ring and the generation that lived under it. Nothing broadcasts a bare year — an instant broadens to a window, a life stays a span — so a click never lights nothing. The connection is always overlap, never coincidence of a single year.

Two holes, avoided

The tempting build that betrays the premise.

Twice in the night the easy path would have hollowed the ship out, and both times the shortcut was refused.

The fabricated frame

The hall named a work of Josephus that does not exist. The quote guard proves the words; nothing yet checks the title, book, chapter or verse that frames them. Recorded as a watch item, not a build — a naive title-guard would flag a real chapter as a fabrication, crying wolf on the hall reading closely. It promotes to a build on a second instance caught on screen.

The planet that spoke

The temptation was to let Jupiter talk — and prose in a planet's mouth is the model's prose dressed as the planet's: a séance with better production values. A planet does not speak in prose. Worse, the first plan baked a static record from SKY.csv when the ship already runs the ephemeris live — Page2 loads astronomy-engine as Sovereign.Ephemeris and computes any classical planet's position for any year in the browser, agreeing with DE422 to better than 99% across the whole historical roster. The planet room is that engine queried live, not a dead copy of it. The law is the feature, not the obstacle.

The principle to keep

The oldest primary documents in the library.

The conjunctions, the gatherings, the risings, the returns — these are not decoration on the timeline. They are read straight from the objects that wrote them, the one witness that has never stopped testifying. A source so primary it needs no voice, because its testimony is its path.

Ask me where I was and I will tell you exactly, from my path. Ask me what it meant and I have no opinion — that is for the ones who watched me.
