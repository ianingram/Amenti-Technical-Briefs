A Paper That Covers Its Own Building

Cover: Arthur and the Round Table — case 3 of 59 on the live docket. A king seated at a table while riders come in over the hill is the summons made literal.

The session began with an instruction to harvest museum plates for article banners. It ended with a court. Nothing in between was a change of plan — each turn was a measurement that contradicted the thing before it, and the last of them found that the strongest surface in the application was the one nobody had built yet.

EVERY FIGURE IN THIS DOCUMENT WAS READ FROM A LIVE WORKER
AT THE TIME OF WRITING. NONE OF THEM IS REMEMBERED.

What changed, in one figure

The application entered this session as a site that generated its content when somebody looked at it and left it unillustrated. It leaves as one that publishes on a schedule, illustrates itself, and reports on its own court.

The session in one frame. Seven rows; six of them were not on the list when it started.

The route, and why it kept turning

A brief that reports only the destination hides the thing worth knowing. Every turn below was a correction, and the correction came from the captain each time. They are recorded because the reasoning is more reusable than the result.

Turn 01The Met, measured and abandonedClosed

The standing instruction was to harvest CC0 museum plates. Four premises in the previous handoff failed within ten minutes of testing — the date filter returns nothing on narrow BCE bands, departmentId collapses a result set, departmentId is not in the object payload at all, and department 3 had been renamed.

What actually stopped it
Not supply — narrative. Subject tags exist and are the right signal, but coverage collapses outside Greek and Roman: 0 of 45 sampled objects tagged in both Medieval and Ancient West Asian. Period matching and narrative content pull against each other and the collection will not give you both.
Turn 02Scenes, keyed to the articleShipped

The obvious replacement was one generated image per figure. That is the restriction, not the solution. Shaka filed two dispatches on consecutive days about different subjects; one image per figure is one image for every article that figure will ever write.

The article knows what it is about. The author key does not.

Turn 03Automation, taken literallyShipped

The first design had 64 images generated by hand and named to match their tags. That is a human matching pictures to words — and it was a design choice, not a constraint. The Worker renders its own.

Turn 04The courtFront page live

The Dispatch was seven figures writing on one theme, which is a magazine. A court paper covers its own building — and the building was already live, with 59 cases, a verdicts table and an argument feed, none of it exposed.

The scene pipeline

Scenes are situations, not figures: burning-library, empty-senate, narrow-pass, moonlit-night. Sixty-four of them. One burning library serves Thoth on Alexandria and Hypatia, which per-figure keying could never do.

Render once per tag, ever. The same shape as the TTS archive already in production.

DISTANT AND UNPEOPLED IS NOT AN AESTHETIC PREFERENCE.
A SILHOUETTE HAS NO LIKENESS TO GET WRONG — SO A BAD MATCH IS A DULL IMAGE
RATHER THAN AN EMBARRASSING ONE. THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT SAFE TO RUN UNWATCHED.

It is the exact inverse of the character portraits, which need the 198×160 preview discipline because the face is the subject. The two pipelines look similar and have opposite tolerances.

What the model actually chose

Thirty-five distinct tags across 77 essays and 18 dispatches — the vocabulary is being used, not collapsing onto a handful.

Selection quality, sampled. The failure this guards against is a pool where one tag absorbs everything.

The covers

An image with a title on it is a poster. What lifts an essay out of a .md file is the type: a hook large enough to carry an argument, and a line lifted from the piece set small beneath it. The scene sits behind, dimmed, and never competes.

All three — sceneTag, hook, pull — are derived from the essay's own text in one call per article. The essays are not rewritten. The model is asked only to point at what is already in them.

What the audit found

A read-only probe walks the paths a reader walks and checks twenty-two invariants against live data. It is GET only by construction — no writes, no generation, no auth, no votes.

THE COST WALL CANNOT TELL A LOAD TEST FROM AN ATTACK.
DAILY_TOKENS IS 2,000,000. TRIPPING IT TAKES THE SITE DOWN FOR REAL READERS
UNTIL 00:00 UTC. A PROBE THAT CAN SPEND CAN CAUSE THE OUTAGE IT MEASURES FOR.

It found, on its first proper run, a live exposure that had been open and unnoticed:

Finding · HIGH · ORIGIN_OPEN

A request bearing Origin: https://not-amenti.example was accepted. ALLOWED_ORIGINS had never been set, so the allowlist fell through to * while the code reported that it had checked. Any website could have spent the Anthropic and Gemini keys from a visitor's browser.

The Worker's own cost-wall header predicted this exactly and said to run wrangler secret list. Nobody had. The probe is what finally checked.

Closing it took two attempts, and the failed one is the more instructive:

A browser sends Origin on cross-origin requests and on POSTs. A same-origin GET sends nothing at all — which is how the site's own feed calls arrive. Treating absent as hostile blanked both publications. Absent is now allowed; wrong is still refused. Nothing is given away: a client that sends no Origin is not a browser, and the caps were always what stopped a person with curl.

Who brings the charges

This was the open question of the session, and the answer was already in the source text rather than needing invention.

The stagger

Each strand a full turn behind the one it reads. The rule is a state, not a duration: it reads a hall that sealed long ago, and it cannot read a half-open one.

Wednesday, not Saturday. The gap after publication is not staleness — it is the voting window.

Verdict → publication wants to be short (old news). Publication → next seal wants to be long (that is when readers act). Wednesday gives a 60-hour-old verdict and four and a half days of participation.

But the clock is the comfort, not the mechanism. The herald refuses to send unless the week manifest reads status: published and done === total. An email cannot be unsent, and a chunked run's likely failure is a half issue — which the old empty-issue guard would have mailed without complaint.

What this session put on the shelf

Thoth's reading room — Budge's Book of the Dead, nine works, Project Gutenberg #7145, public domain. Budge titles his fourth chapter "Thoth, the Author of the Book of the Dead", so the attribution is sourced rather than asserted.

Chapter CXXV is the centre of the shelf, and the fit is uncanny: the Hall of Maati, Osiris presiding, the Forty-Two assessors who "keep ward over sinners" on "the day of estimating characters", the balance, and Amemit — Budge's spelling — crouched beneath it.

The room note names Budge as a problem, not just a source: 1890s readings, superseded in places, Faulkner is the modern standard. Two earlier reading-room notes carried plausible facts absorbed from the wrong author. The shelf says plainly what it is.
