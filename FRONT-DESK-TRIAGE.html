Front Desk Triage

A visitor asks one question. Behind the desk, several faculties can answer — and each is bound by a different rule for what it may say. How the Ask Amenti box decides, and shows its work.

The desk does not just answer. It says where the answer came from, and refuses to let one faculty borrow another’s authority.
— the discipline of the desk

The problem

One box, many faculties.

A visitor types a question into one box. But the ship can answer it from several places, and they are not equal. A primary-source room quotes verbatim, cited. The ship’s own files describe the architecture. General knowledge supplies the connective tissue no source aboard yet covers. And the mention graph, newest of all, knows whom a figure named. Each of these may say some things and must never say others — and the danger is that one faculty borrows the authority of another: general knowledge wearing the colours of a citation, a guess dressed as a quote.

Triage is how the desk keeps them honest — routing the question, answering from what fits, and showing which faculty spoke so the visitor always knows the ground beneath each sentence.

The lanes

Four faculties, four rules.

The engine hands the desk a response, and the desk reads which faculties spoke — opened (rooms), register (ship files), the prose itself (scaffolding), and now the graph. Each renders in its own panel, under its own rule.

ROOMS
Primary sources, quoted verbatim. Rule: every quotation is checked against the fetched text (the quote guard). A verified quote earns gold; a substituted word fails and is not shown as a quote. The read from panel lists every work opened — including those read but not quoted, so coverage is never overstated.
REGISTER
The ship’s own files. Rule: a question about the architecture is answered from the documents that describe it, and those documents are named as what was opened. The ship’s files are a thing that can be cited too.
SCAFFOLDING
General knowledge — the connective tissue. Rule: context (dates, places, background) is allowed in the prose, but never wears the citation colours and never claims a source it lacks. It is scaffolding: provisional, retreating as sources rise to replace it. (The ratchet, Slip #55.)
THE GRAPH
Whom a figure named — the newest lane. Rule: when a room is opened, its harvested edges appear as the connections: whom the figure named, in their own text, and — where the named figure also has a room — a door the reader can walk through. A connection is shown only because a source bears it.

The failure lane

The honest “no.”

Triage includes the case where no faculty can answer well. The desk does not paper over it. If the router misses, if no room opens and no register file fits, the desk says so — degraded carries the reason, and the answer is not dressed up as more than it is. A silent fallback that quietly downgrades a sourced answer to a remembered one is the failure the whole ship is built to prevent; the desk names the downgrade instead of hiding it.

Better a visible “I could not source that” than an invisible guess in a citation’s clothing.

Why it holds together

The desk borrows no authority it has not earned.

  • Each lane renders separately. Rooms in the answer and the read from panel; the graph in the connections; scaffolding in plain prose. A reader can always tell which faculty is speaking, because each has its own place and its own colour.
  • The quote guard is sovereign and untouched. Every lane added — colouring, connections — is built to leave the verification byte-for-byte intact. New faculties may be added; none may weaken the one that proves the quotes.
  • The newest lane changed nothing beneath it. The connections panel reads harvested edges the desk already had the key for and renders them in their own panel — additive, separate, sourced. The proof that this was done safely: the guard did not move.
  • Gold is reserved. Across every lane, the earned colour of a verified quotation is never spent on anything else — not a date, not a connection, not a name. The one thing that means “checked against the text” is never faked.
The front desk is where the whole ship meets the visitor. Its triage is the promise that every sentence carries the weight of its own source — no more, and no less.
