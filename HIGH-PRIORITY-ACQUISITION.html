What this is

A roster placement is not a room. These earn both.

Tonight’s expansion added many souls as placements — a dated bar on the timeline, a name among the living, an anchor for the mention graph. The kings of Rome are placements: legendary, no works, they do not speak. These 123 are different. Every one is a real author whose writing survives in the public domain, cross-referenced tonight to a Project Gutenberg author record. They are not placements — they are room-worthy, and this brief names them as the priority acquisition: the worklist for turning roster rows into reading rooms.

They were found, not remembered. The batch is the intersection of two structured facts — who wrote (Wikidata) and whose work we can hold (Gutenberg) — filtered to antiquity, where survival is its own significance test. There are no minor authors here, because the minor ancients did not survive to have a record. THE STANDING SLIP #54, #55.

The rule that governs the build

A room is earned, one edition at a time.

  • Each is a citation job, not a copy. The Gutenberg link proves works EXIST; the room requires each work cited to its edition and graded — the same discipline as every room aboard.
  • Some are lost or partial. Varro survives only in part; much of the tradition is fragments. The room states what survives, honestly, the way a good room always has.
  • These feed the mention graph (#47): Livy names the kings, Suetonius the Caesars, Josephus his contemporaries. A cohort that cites each other is where the citable-mention layer pays off first.
  • The order is the priority. Oldest first — the deepest authors anchor the most of the timeline, and the earliest rooms enrich the most panels.
The roster gave them a place in time tonight. The library owes them their voice — found in a real edition, cited to its page, quoted only from the text.
