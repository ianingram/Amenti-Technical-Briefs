Amenti Studios · The offline mixer

TheMIXMASTER

Why Recording Failed · The Workflow · What It Mixes · The Proof · Where It Lives

An Amenti production is not a sound file. It is a cue sheet, a cast, a sound sheet and a speech engine, put together by the player as it plays. To make a road track for the car, that performance had to be recorded as it played, in real time, a forty-minute chapter in forty minutes or more, on a machine that could stop at any moment. On the night of the twelfth of November it stopped again and again. On the evening of the thirteenth the recording was replaced with a mixer: the same lines and the same rules, assembled on a clock of its own, a forty-minute chapter in under three minutes.

THE MIX IS BUILT, NOT RECORDED.
THE SAME LINES FROM THE SAME CACHE, THE SAME RULES AS THE PLAYER.
NOTHING IS VOICED TWICE. NOTHING PLAYS OUT LOUD.

I · Why recording failed

Recording meant opening the player in a browser, letting it perform, and capturing the sound as it came out. Over two nights it finished 17 of Dracula's 27 chapters. These are the things that went wrong:

FailureWhat happened
Real timeEvery chapter took at least its own length to capture. Ten more chapters meant another night.
Out of memoryAn encoding step held a whole chapter in memory and was killed, taking a recording with it.
The two-hour limitEach background run in the cloud workspace stops at two hours. A chapter cut off at that point was lost and had to start again.
A restartThe cloud workspace restarted in the middle of the day and stopped the chapters being recorded.
A silent stallChapter XV never finished. One line in it would not play, and the player waited for it forever while the background sound ran on.

Every finished chapter was kept, because each was backed up the moment it was done. What was lost was time, and time was the problem.

II · The workflow

Fig. 1 · The workflow: three inputs, four steps, two private destinations

1 · The voice, from the cache
The voice service charges for each new recording of a line, so the mixer must ask for each line in exactly the same words, voice and style the player uses. If one byte differs, the line is recorded and paid for again. So the mixer doesn't contain its own copy of the voice code. It opens the real player page and lets the real engine cut every line into its measures and request them, and it keeps what comes back. Every line already heard on the website comes straight from the cache.
2 · The mix, by the same rules
The player's sound code (sound.js) is a set of rules: when the background fades in, how far it drops under a voice, how long a crossfade lasts, where on its words a sound effect lands. mix.py follows those rules on its own clock, measure by measure, with the same fades and the same curves. Even the small random variations are the same: each door or wolf howl gets the same pitch and speed it gets in the browser.
3 · The finish
The same finishing settings as the recorded road tracks: start trimmed, loudness levelled to the standard for spoken audio, a short fade in, two seconds of quiet at the end, and the chapter's number, title and album written into the file, so a car's screen shows "Chapter 15 · Dracula · Amenti Studios".

III · What it mixes

Fig. 2 · The five layers, placed exactly as the player places them

LayerHow it is placed
VoiceEach measure back to back, followed by its own pause: longer after a sentence, longest after a paragraph. Lines recorded beforehand as finished files play as they are.
BackgroundOne recording looped three ways, at 37, 53 and 71 seconds and slightly different speeds, so it never repeats audibly. It sits down under the voices. In 4 s, crossfade 8 s, out 6 s.
ScoreLooped at its own speed and its own level. It sits down less than the background and slowly swells and ebbs. In 6 s, crossfade 10 s, out 8 s. Pieces of equal length stay in step. Only a "cut" (the Demeter) stops it dead.
FillsPiano under a letter read aloud, while the score steps back to a third of its level.
Sound effectsAt the start of a line, or on the words they belong to, placed inside the measure that holds those words.

V · What it found

Because the mixer works line by line, a line that fails is named instead of hidden. Its first run found chapter XV's stall straight away: line 86 is a single dash, a line with no words. The voice service can't voice it, so the live player waited there forever. The mixer skips it and reports it. The website player still needs a small fix, either in the cue sheet or in the player, so that a listener never meets the same silence.

The workflow runs in Amenti-Readings because the cue sheets, sounds and player are there, and the bucket's keys were added there on the twelfth. A private repository would get smaller machines (2 processors) and a monthly allowance of minutes. Being public also means anything it saves on GitHub can be seen. So no finished chapter is ever saved on GitHub. Each machine sends its chapter straight to the private bucket. The logs show only chapter numbers and times.

Fig. 4 · One Run, twenty machines, nothing paid left in public

To run it: open Amenti-Readings, go to the Actions tab, choose the mixer, enter the book and the chapters, and press Run. It works from a phone.

The deeper change is to how a book is made. With a chapter rebuilt in minutes, a note like "the wind is too loud in chapter 9" becomes a change to the sound sheet and a new chapter the same evening. That is what makes the plan Ian set on the thirteenth practical: finish the recordings, review them by ear, then illustrate.
