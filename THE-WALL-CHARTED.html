Nobody wrote a bad line

The hall declared every document on every question, so that nothing could be missed in silence. That was right. It was also linear, and the corpus grew.

Each document cost about 85 characters of prompt. One of them crossed the wall, and after that the hall answered nothing. It was found by building an instrument, not by a visitor. Under the doors the line is flat: a new document inside an existing section costs nothing.

Trimming was never the answer

The obvious fix is shorter descriptions. It buys weeks, and it dies against what the box is for — Ask Amenti has to reach the architecture, the library and the roster together.

1,751 entries: 190 documents, 550 works, 1,011 souls. Delete every description and it still overruns three times. Logarithmic scale — a linear one would put the available space at two pixels.

There is no trim, and no larger wall, that reaches it. The declare-everything design was finished. The only question left was what replaced it.

Doors, not leaves

The corpus already had a shape. The library is not 550 loose works — it is 52 rooms. The documents are not 191 loose files — they are 8 sections. So the hall declares the doors and opens what a question reaches.

Each room shows up to three of its own section titles. That costs 3,444 characters over a bare list, and it is the difference between a lookup and a search.

Asked which souls wrote about betrayal, the hall reached Brutus. The word “betrayal” appears nowhere in the doors. It arrived through The overthrow and The price — two of his section titles. Fragment search could not have done it; there is no fragment to match.

Two calls, four shapes

The first call sees only the doors and answers which to open. The second sees what was opened and answers the visitor — and does not carry the door list, which is what pays for the passages.

The probe measured three of these for a day and the missing one was the largest. Now all four are measured, and the probe's worst case agrees with a live run to the character.

Ten constants against one wall

Every attempt on 1 September to change one of these by reasoning produced a warning or a breach. Writing the quoting rule properly cost 750 characters and the passages paid. The navigation cost 905 and the passages paid again. Sending bare doors in the fallback returned 3,444 and the passages took it back.

Do not tune a constant here by reasoning. Run the probe.

The instrument is what makes this affordable

Ten budgets across four prompt shapes is more than anyone should hold in their head. It is only safe because something measures it — and that something was attacked until it admitted six faults.

  • A gate that could not fire. It exited non-zero only under a flag no workflow passed.
  • A green light on a decoy. A compiling fake catalogue builder measured 11,060 characters and passed.
  • A crash instead of a report. A private constant the lift did not take. Five times since, a new one has broken it — and each time it said UNREAD rather than passing.
  • A truncated library passing. It took its expected room count from the file it was measuring, so the expectation shrank with the evidence.
  • Three separate under-reports — 160 characters, then 477, then 285 — each because it measured a model of the prompt instead of the prompt.
A probe that constructs its own worst case is measuring its author's imagination. It now builds every worst case from the hall's own functions on the live registers. Probe and live agree to the character.

It has fired both ways in production: red at 12:24 on 31 August when the hall did not fit, green at 22:32 when it did. A gate that can only fire is not a gate.
